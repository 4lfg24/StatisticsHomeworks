Exploiting Plaintext Redundancy to Recover Caesar's Shift Key

Natural language is highly non-uniform and redundant: in English, the letter E occurs with probability ~12.7%, while Z occurs with probability ~0.07%. Because the Caesar cipher applies a deterministic cyclic translation Cᵢ ≡ (Pᵢ + k) mod 26, it completely preserves this relative frequency profile, shifting it by exactly k positions. This laboratory demonstrates how to automatically recover the secret shift key using the Chi-squared Goodness-of-Fit test, Frequency Cross-Correlation, and Argmax Peak Matching across ciphertexts of varying lengths.

Academic Discussion

Effectiveness, Unicity Distance & Statistical Limitations

A rigorous discussion of why the algorithm works, when it fails, and how information theory explains its performance boundaries.

Shannon's Unicity Distance (Theory vs Practice)

Claude Shannon defined the Unicity Distance U as the minimum length of ciphertext required for a computationally unbounded adversary to uniquely determine the secret key without ambiguity:

U = H(𝒦) / D = log₂(26) / [R_L · log₂(26)] ≈ 4.70 / 3.20 ≈ 1.5 to 2 letters

Theoretically, just 2 letters of English text contain enough statistical redundancy to rule out 25 spurious keys. However, unicity distance assumes an optimal language model with infinite n-gram memory. In practice, unigram statistical tests (like Chi-squared) suffer from sample variance on short snippets. Our empirical benchmark proves that N ≥ 25–30 characters is required for near-100% confidence.

Algorithm Comparison: Chi-Squared vs Argmax

• Chi-Squared (χ²): Considers all 26 letter frequencies simultaneously. Even if the letter 'E' happens to be rare in a short sentence, the combined frequencies of 'T', 'A', 'O', 'I', 'N', and rare letters ('Q', 'Z') collectively guide the optimization to the correct shift.

• Argmax Peak Matching: Relies solely on the mode (the single highest peak). In short texts (e.g., $N < 25$), words like "BANANA" or "MISSISSIPPI" create local modes on 'A' or 'S', causing Argmax to fail dramatically.

• Cross-Correlation: Highly robust and mathematically equivalent to maximizing the cosine similarity between empirical and theoretical probability vectors.

Pathological Failure Modes & Limitations

Statistical cryptanalysis fails or degrades under specific conditions:

1. Lipograms: Texts deliberately crafted to omit specific letters (e.g. Ernest Vincent Wright's 50,000-word novel Gadsby contains zero instances of 'E'). Both Argmax and Chi-squared will misidentify the shift unless tailored to lipogram models.
2. Technical/Domain Vocabularies: Medical, chemical, or algebraic formulas with abnormal concentrations of 'X', 'Z', 'C', or 'K'.
3. Cross-Language Mismatch: Attacking an Italian or Spanish ciphertext using English reference tables will misalign vowels ('A', 'I', 'O'), producing distorted χ² minima.
