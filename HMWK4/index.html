The Geometry of Random Walks:
LLN, Variance Scaling & The Central Limit Theorem

Consider n enterprise systems under attack by m independent attackers. We model the adversarial intrusion process as a discrete-time stochastic process with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) increments X₁, X₂, …, Xₙ. We contrast the asymmetric Bernoulli model (Xᵢ ∈ {0, 1}, success probability p) with the symmetric zero-mean Rademacher model (Xᵢ ∈ {−1, +1}, probability 1/2), tracing the fundamental trichotomy of variance across total, average, and diffusive normalizations.

Mathematical Foundations & Security Architecture Analysis

Mathematical Foundations Stochastic Modeling

1. Stochastic Modeling of the Penetration Process

Let (Ω, ℱ, ℙ) be a complete probability space equipped with discrete-time filtration ℱₙ = σ(X₁, …, Xₙ) representing the historical information of the penetration process up to step n. We model the adversarial penetration score as a sum of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random increments:

Sₙ = ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ, \quad S₀ = 0

Two distinct operational models govern the increment Xᵢ:

  • Bernoulli Model (Xᵢ ∈ {0, 1}): Models binary exploit attempts where 1 represents a compromised target node and 0 indicates a thwarted attempt. The expected value is E[Xᵢ] = μ = p and the single-step variance is Var(Xᵢ) = σ² = p(1−p). Because p > 0, the trajectory exhibits an intrinsic linear upward drift.
  • Rademacher Model (Xᵢ ∈ {−1, +1}): Models a symmetric, zero-sum adversarial game where +1 denotes an attacker foothold and −1 denotes an automated honeypot trap or blue-team quarantine event. Increments have equal probability ℙ(Xᵢ = +1) = ℙ(Xᵢ = −1) = 1/2. The expected value is E[Xᵢ] = μ = 0 and the variance is Var(Xᵢ) = σ² = 1.
\text{Martingale Property (Rademacher):} \quad E[Sₙ₊₁ \mid ℱₙ] = Sₙ + E[Xₙ₊₁] = Sₙ

The Rademacher trajectory forms a classical discrete-time martingale: given current state Sₙ, the best forecast for any future score is precisely Sₙ.

Variance Scaling Asymptotic Orders

2. Normalizations & The Trichotomy of Variance

By applying scaling factor n⁻ᵅ to the cumulative sum Sₙ, we uncover three fundamentally distinct asymptotic behaviors as n → ∞:

\text{For } Yₙ = n^{-α} Sₙ: \quad Var(Yₙ) = Var\left(n^{-α} ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ\right) = n^{-2α} ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Var(Xᵢ) = σ² n^{1 - 2α}
  • Case α = 0 (Total Score Sₙ): Exponent 1 − 2(0) = +1. The variance grows linearly: Var(Sₙ) = n σ² = O(n). The standard deviation scales as SD(Sₙ) = σ √n, creating a trumpet-shaped confidence funnel that widens indefinitely. Individual trajectories diverge widely.
  • Case α = 1 (Average Score Sₙ/n): Exponent 1 − 2(1) = −1. The variance decays inversely: Var(Sₙ/n) = σ²/n = O(1/n). As n → ∞, the variance vanishes to zero, forcing all sample paths to collapse onto the constant mean μ.
  • Case α = 1/2 (Normalized Score Sₙ/√n): Exponent 1 − 2(1/2) = 0. The variance is completely invariant: Var(Sₙ/√n) = σ² = O(1). The scale of random fluctuations remains perfectly stable across all horizons n, establishing the exact diffusive balance required for the Central Limit Theorem.
Convergence in Probability Chebyshev Bound

3. Law of Large Numbers (LLN) & Convergence in Probability

The Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN) states that the empirical sample mean S̄ₙ = Sₙ/n converges in probability to the theoretical expectation μ:

\forall ε > 0, \quad \lim_{n \to \infty} ℙ\left(\left|\frac{Sₙ}{n} - μ\right| \ge ε\right) = 0

This convergence can be rigorously bounded in finite samples using Chebyshev's Inequality:

ℙ\left(\left|\frac{Sₙ}{n} - μ\right| \ge ε\right) \le \frac{Var(Sₙ/n)}{ε²} = \frac{σ²}{n ε²}

In our simulation console, we empirically verify this bound across all m attackers at each step k. The empirical violation fraction P̂(|S̄ₖ − μ| ≥ ε) remains strictly below the hyperbolic curve σ²/(k ε²), vanishing asymptotically to 0. Furthermore, by Kolmogorov's Strong Law of Large Numbers, the convergence holds almost surely: ℙ(\lim_{n \to \infty} S̄ₙ = μ) = 1.

Convergence in Distribution Central Limit Theorem

4. Central Limit Theorem & The Rademacher Invariance

The Lindeberg-Lévy Central Limit Theorem (CLT) dictates that the standardized sum of i.i.d. finite-variance random variables converges in distribution to a standard Gaussian:

Zₙ^* = \frac{Sₙ - n μ}{σ \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty

Here lies the profound elegance of the Rademacher model: Because μ = 0 and σ = 1, the raw normalized total score is already identically equal to the standardized variable:

\frac{Sₙ}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{Sₙ - n(0)}{1 \cdot \sqrt{n}} = Zₙ^* \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)

In contrast, for the Bernoulli model, Sₙ/√n = p√n + √{p(1−p)} Zₙ^*. The raw normalized score drifts towards +∞ at rate p√n; normality only emerges once the centering term n μ is subtracted.

Moreover, by the Berry-Esseen Theorem, the uniform convergence rate to normality is:

\sup_{z \in \mathbb{R}} |Fₙ(z) - \Phi(z)| \le \frac{C \cdot E[|X - μ|³]}{σ³ \sqrt{n}}

In the Rademacher model, E[|X|³] = 1 and the third central moment E[X³] = 0 (zero skewness). The absence of skewness eliminates the leading asymmetric error term, causing Rademacher distributions to lock onto the Gaussian bell curve with remarkable speed even for modest system counts n ≈ 30.

Cybersecurity & Operational Risk Synthesis: System Size n vs Attacker Collective m

The statistical behaviors revealed above directly inform enterprise defense sizing, threat modeling, and incident response engineering:

1. System Horizon (n Targets) — Predictability of Density:

As an enterprise grows from n = 50 to n = 2000 workstations, the absolute volume of successful breaches Sₙ ≈ n p escalates linearly. Zero breaches is statistically impossible. However, the per-system breach density Sₙ/n → p becomes almost entirely deterministic by the LLN. Security executives can budget incident triage capacity based on the stable constant rate p rather than erratic random spikes. In the Rademacher paradigm (where honeypots inflict −1 penalties), the expected breach balance is zero, but diffusive drift ±√n means large networks will still suffer severe transient breach excursions that require automated quarantine.

2. Attacker Collective (m Actors) — The Tyranny of Extreme Values:

In an attack ecosystem with m concurrent adversaries (e.g. automated botnets, credential-stuffing campaigns), aggregate organizational exposure scales as T_{m,n} = ∑ⱼ Sₙ⁽ʲ⁾ = m n p. Crucially, cyber defenders are breached by the worst-case attacker, not the average attacker! According to Extreme Value Theory (EVT), the maximum penetration score across m adversaries scales as:
E[\max_{1 \le j \le m} Sₙ⁽ʲ⁾] \approx n p + σ \sqrt{2 n \ln m}.
Even if individual adversary capability is modest (p = 0.15), a collective of m = 3000 attackers guarantees that at least one adversary will achieve an extreme multi-sigma penetration depth. Enterprise defense must therefore architect for tail percentiles (99.9th percentile threat actors) via Zero-Trust microsegmentation.
